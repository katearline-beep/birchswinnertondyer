**2026-10-19. Source-based reconstruction of published mathematics, not a new theorem.** Primary source: Douglas Ulmer, [*Elliptic curves with large rank over function fields*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109163), Annals of Mathematics **155** (2002), 295–315. ## 1. A complete explicit geometric map identity — over the integers

Let \(F_d\subset\mathbb P^3\) be the Fermat surface \(x_0^d+x_1^d+x_2^d+x_3^d=0\) and let \(W_d\) be the Weierstrass surface defined by \(Y^2Z+XYZ-X^3+Z^3t^d=0\).

Ulmer §4.2 gives, on the indicated dense rational chart,
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=(x_0x_1x_2)^d,\\
Y&=(x_0^2x_2)^d,\\
Z&=-x_1^{3d},\\
t&=\frac{x_0^3x_2^2x_3}{x_1^6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substitution yields the exact Laurent-polynomial identity **over \(\mathbb Z\)**:
\[
\boxed{Y^2Z+XYZ-X^3+Z^3t^d
  =-(x_0^3x_1^3x_2^2)^d
       (x_0^d+x_1^d+x_2^d+x_3^d).}
\]
The proof is four independent monomial identities and needs **no** finite-field enumeration or special value of \(p\). Our [exact monomial verifier](ulmer_fermat_quotient.py) checks the four exponents, a deliberately corrupted exponent in \(t\), and actual smooth-chart points in characteristic five and seven. For \(p=5,d=6\), Fermat \([1:1:2:2]\) maps to the affine Weierstrass point \((x,y,t)=(1,1,3)\); for \(p=7,d=8\), \([2:1:1:1]\) maps to \((3,5,1)\).

For a primitive \(d\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), the two diagonal automorphisms
\[
g_1=(\zeta^2,\zeta,1,1),\quad g_2=(1,\zeta,\zeta^3,1)
\]
generate Ulmer's symmetry subgroup \(\Gamma\). Every displayed rational function is invariant; the verifier enumerates the action exponents and checks \(|\Gamma|=d^2\) for \(d=6,8,26\). **Ulmer proves** that \(F_d/\Gamma\) is birational to \(W_d\), via the field-degree comparison: \([k(F_d):k(F_1)]=d^3\), while \([k(W_d):k(F_1)]=d\), so the intermediate extension has degree \(d^2\). The identity, symmetry invariance and finite group-order enumeration are independently checked here, but the generality of the degree/birationality theorem is **cited from Ulmer** rather than newly proved by our short script.

**Major strategic observation:** the *rational-map identity itself* survives base change to characteristic zero, since it is a polynomial identity over \(\mathbb Z\). What does **not** transfer is the finite-characteristic theorem relating the Fermat surface's Frobenius/Gauss-sum eigenvalues, algebraic cycles and analytic zeros. Ulmer explicitly notes his corresponding \(\mathbb Q(t)\) family has rank bounded independently of \(d\). Therefore an algebraic correspondence, **by itself**, cannot be the missing BSD bridge over \(\mathbb Q\).

## 2. Exact correction: base-field versus geometric Mordell–Weil ranks

**A serious error was detected in the previous Session 19 notes (C-0016), which conflated the ranks over \(\mathbb F_p(t)\) with Ulmer's larger rank over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p(t)\).** Ulmer Theorem **9.2** gives the exact rank over *each* \(\mathbb F_q(t)\), where \(q\) is a power of \(p\); Remark **1.6(2)** gives the rank over \(\mathbb F_{p^{2n}}(t)\), equal to the geometric rank, not in general the prime-field rank.

For our selected \(d=p^n+1\):

| p, n, d | Rank \(\mathbb F_p(t)\), by Ulmer Thm 9.2 | Rank \(\mathbb F_{p^{2n}}(t)\) and geometric rank, by Ulmer Remark 1.6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5, 1, 6 | **2** | **3** |
| 7, 1, 8 | **3** | **7** |
| 5, 2, 26 | **7** | **25** |

The published exact-rank theorem was always available; the incorrect field label was our mistake and is not an issue with Ulmer's mathematics. **Any reference in historical Session 19 prose to ranks (3,7,25) *over Fp(t)* must be read as the corrected (2,3,7).** We preserve the historical correction here and in the claim ledger, harness and review, rather than suppressing it.

### Global Frobenius orbit computation (not mere local point counting)

Ulmer §9.1 uses the invariants of the Fermat surface's \(\Gamma\)-quotient. For \(d\mid p^n+1\), the relevant character group is generated by \((3,-6,2,1)\pmod d\). A character with multiplier \(a\) contributes when \(a=0\) or \(6a\not\equiv0\pmod d\). Partition those multipliers into Frobenius orbits \(a\mapsto qa\bmod d\); let \(o_q(a)\) denote each orbit length.

Using the **published** Jacobi-sum identity from Ulmer §8.1, all relevant Jacobi eigenvalues have the expected power of \(q\). His Corollary 7.7 then gives the actual global \(\Gamma\)-quotient surface cohomology factor:
\[
\boxed{P_2(F_d/\Gamma,T)=\prod_{\mathcal O}
(1-q^{|\mathcal O|}T^{|\mathcal O|}).}
\]
Every orbit contributes exactly one factor vanishing at \(T=1/q\); the pole order of the quotient surface's zeta function is the **number of orbits**, not the number of admissible characters. Ulmer Proposition 6.4 and §9.1 add the special-fibre corrections:
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q(t)}E_d
=\#\{\text{Frobenius orbits}\}-1+c_2+c_3,
\]
where \(c_2=1\) iff \(2\mid d\) and \(4\mid q-1\); \(c_3=0\) if \(3\nmid d\), \(1\) if \(3\mid d\) but \(3\nmid q-1\), and \(2\) if \(3\mid d\) and \(3\mid q-1\).

The [stdlib Frobenius-orbit verifier](ulmer_frobenius_orbits.py) enumerates all relevant characters and the orbits, constructs the **global \(P_2\)** polynomial coefficient-by-coefficient, then applies the published corrections. At the prime field it gives:

- \(p=5,d=6\): one orbit, \(P_2=1-5T\), special-fibre corrections \(c_2=1,c_3=1\), rank \(1-1+1+1=\mathbf2\).
- \(p=7,d=8\): four orbits of lengths \(1,2,2,2\), \(P_2=(1-7T)(1-49T^2)^3\), corrections zero, rank \(\mathbf3\).
- \(p=5,d=26\): seven orbits of lengths \(1,4,4,4,4,4,4\), \(P_2=(1-5T)(1-625T^4)^6\), \(c_2=1,c_3=0\), rank \(\mathbf7\).

After extending constants to \(q=p^{2n}\), \(q\equiv1\bmod d\), all character orbits have size one; special-fibre corrections now yield \(\mathbf3,\mathbf7,\mathbf{25}\). These are **theorem-dependent cohomological character computations**, much more informative than our previous local fibre counts. The software does **not independently evaluate Jacobi sums or prove the Tate conjecture**; those fundamental eigenvalue and BSD results are Ulmer's published inputs. This is nevertheless the first reconstruction of a **global zeta-function factor** within our project.

### What the discrepancy teaches us

Arithmetic rank can increase after extending the constant field even when the geometric surface and its algebraic cycles are unchanged. **Frobenius orbits, not merely the number of geometric characters, control which classes contribute over a given field.** The geometric \(\Gamma\)-quotient correspondence exists over \(\mathbb Z\), but the characteristic-\(p\) Frobenius/Jacobi and Tate mechanisms cannot simply be re-labeled as a number-field proof.

Our next genuinely open challenge is not a larger table of finite fibres. It is to isolate a **characteristic-zero cohomological object and a proven comparison to the central derivatives of the original complex \(L(E/\mathbb Q,s)\)**, with the characteristic-dependent step made explicit and a rank-two negative control.

## 3. An independent global Jacobi eigenvalue, beyond the published-orbit formula (C-0018)

The [independent F49 character enumerator](ulmer_jacobi_f49.py) reconstructs a **nontrivial** factor of the p7,d8 global quotient cohomology without relying on Ulmer's *evaluation* of this particular Jacobi sum. For Γ-perpendicular character \(a=(3,-6,2,1)\bmod8=(3,2,2,1)\), the q=7 Frobenius orbit has length \(u(a)=2\); the character values live in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\) and the defining finite field is \(\mathbb F_{49}\).

Construct \(\mathbb F_{49}=\mathbb F_7[i]/(i^2+1)\) explicitly, find a multiplicative primitive generator \(g\) of order 48 and define \(\chi(g)=\zeta_8\). For \((x_0,x_1,x_2)\in(\mathbb F_{49}^{\times})^3\), the equation \(x_3=-x_0-x_1-x_2\) determines the fourth coordinate; omit the cases \(x_3=0\). The enumerated sum is *exactly*
\[
\sum_{\substack{x_0,\ldots,x_3\in\mathbb F_{49}^{\times}\\x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3=0}}
\chi(x_0)^3\chi(x_1)^2\chi(x_2)^2\chi(x_3).
\]
After reducing by \(\zeta_8^4=-1\), the four rational integer coefficients in the basis \(1,\zeta_8,\zeta_8^2,\zeta_8^3\) are
\[
\boxed{(2352,0,0,0)=48(49,0,0,0).}
\]
The number of admissible triples is exactly \(48^3-(48^2-48)=108336\). Ulmer §7 defines \(J(a)\) by dividing this sum by \(q^{u(a)}-1=48\), so
\[
\boxed{J(3,2,2,1)=49=7^2}.
\]
This independently verifies the specific **nontrivial Frobenius eigenvalue** predicted by Ulmer's Proposition 8.1 and used by one of the three factors \((1-49T^2)\) in the previously computed global \(P_2(F_8/\Gamma,T)\). The verifier repeats the complete character sum with a different primitive generator, checks the total admissible-triple count and rejects a four-character tuple whose product is not the identity.

**Claim boundary:** one verified Jacobi eigenvalue is not the full Jacobi-sum theorem for all characters, not an independent proof of the surface Tate conjecture, not a proof of BSD over \(\mathbb F_7(t)\), and certainly not a theorem about the original **complex** \(L''(E/\mathbb Q,1)\).

**Research decision:** The next experiment can target a different character orbit (e.g. p5,d26, requiring \(\mathbb F_{625}\)) or, with higher priority, the **algebraic-cycle/Tate-to-zeta** step and what a proposed characteristic-zero motivic regulator would actually need to prove. Do not repeat this success as an assertion that a number-field BSD comparison now exists.
