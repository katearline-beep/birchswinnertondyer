# Proof laboratory A: what rank-two generalized Kato theory actually says

**Status (2026-10-19): primary-source theorem applicability audit, not a new theorem, a Kato class computation, or a complex BSD proof.** Chief sources: Castella–Hsieh, *On the nonvanishing of generalised Kato classes for elliptic curves of rank 2*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 10 (2022), e12, [published text](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2021.85), [author PDF](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/Kato.pdf). Companion work: Castella, [*Nonvanishing of generalised Kato classes and Iwasawa main conjectures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01481).

## Two very different published theorems — do not conflate them

**Theorem A: rank-two nonvanishing criterion.** For \(p>3\) good ordinary, complex root sign \(+1\), \(L(E,1)=0\), suitable weight-one \(g\), a nonzero auxiliary adjoint central value \(L(E,\mathrm{ad}^0(g),1)\), mod-\(p\) irreducibility, an **odd** number of inert primes in \(N^-\) (with squarefree \(N^-\)), and ramification conditions at these bad inert primes, the relevant nonzero generalized Kato class \(\kappa_{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}}\) implies \(\dim_{\mathbb Q_p}\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb Q,V_pE)=2\). The hypotheses also govern the theorem's converse criteria. This is not a theorem asserting the existence or nonvanishing of \(\kappa\) for arbitrary rank.

**Kummer obstruction in rank four.** The standard rational Kummer map injects \(E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb Q_p\) into \(\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb Q,V_pE)\), so our known four independent rational points give \(\dim_{\mathbb Q_p}\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb Q,V_pE)\ge4\). Therefore, **if all Theorem A hypotheses could be checked for a chosen auxiliary \(g,K,p\)**, the contrapositive forces this particular \(\kappa_{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}}=0\) for our curve. That is a *conditional deduction from a published theorem*, **not** evidence that all generalized Kato classes vanish, a result on complex rank, or permission to apply Theorem A outside its hypotheses.

**The current \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})\) fails Theorem A's odd-inert setup:** its fundamental discriminant \(-7\equiv1\bmod8\) makes 2 split, and the quadratic residue \(({-7}/117223)=+1\) makes the other conductor prime split. Hence \(N^-=1\), containing **zero** inert conductor primes, not an odd number. A theorem whose odd-inert hypothesis fails gives **no** implication about nonzero or zero \(\kappa\) for this K. Testing nonvanishing at our old K by citing Theorem A would be invalid.

**Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 4.5: arbitrary-rank derived heights already exist.** Theorem 4.1 presents the decreasing Selmer filtration \(S_p^{(r)}(E/K)\) and higher derived pairings \(h_p^{(r)}\), with \(S_p^{(r+1)}\) equal to the null-space of \(h_p^{(r)}\), under its own *different* hypotheses (good ordinary \(p\), discriminant coprime \(Np\), squarefree inert conductor factor \(N^-\), and residual ramification at the relevant primes). Unlike Theorem A's generalized-Kato nonvanishing conclusion, the filtration's construction **is not restricted to algebraic rank two**. Theorem 4.5 gives a specialized formula relating derived heights of suitable global classes to Coleman maps and local logarithms *when the class's Iwasawa-level hypotheses are met*. **It does not magically manufacture the required classes or prove original complex \(L''\) vanishing.**

This corrects a strategic misconception from the previous plan: **we should not try to invent arbitrary-rank derived-height theory as though it did not exist.** We should audit the domain of Theorem 4.5 (actual global Iwasawa class, localization, and augmentation-order hypotheses) and determine whether a precisely defined useful class can be constructed in rank four. Theorem A's nonvanishing obstruction implies that a naive reuse of its rank-two class is *not* the required rank-four object.
