## 1. Exact improvement: two local logarithms mod \(11^4\)

The short-Weierstrass invariant differential is \(\omega_s=dZ/(2W)\); the normalized local logarithms are \(\lambda_\pm(Q)=\log_{E_0,\omega_s}(Q_\pm)/11\). With \(11\) a split prime of good reduction and \(\#E(\mathbb F_{11})=18\), \([18]Q\) is in the formal group. Its parameter \(t=-Z/W\) satisfies \(\log_{E_0}(t)=t+O(t^5)\). At our precision \(11^4\) *after dividing by 11*, all higher terms disappear, so
\[
\lambda_\pm(Q)\equiv\frac{t([18]Q_\pm)}{18\cdot 11}\pmod{11^4}.
\]
For the minimal differential on the original model, \(\omega_{\min}=dx/(2y+x)=2\omega_s\); thus minimal-model logarithms are twice the displayed quantities. Because \(2\) is an 11-adic unit, all kernels and primitivity conclusions are unchanged, but numeric normalization must be converted in any comparison with height literature.

Hensel roots of \(-7\) mod \(11^5=161051\) are
\(w_+=156785\) (the root \(\equiv2\pmod{11}\)) and \(w_-=4266\).
The exact computations give:

| Point | \(\lambda_+\bmod14641\) | \(\lambda_-\bmod14641\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(P_1\) | 7462 | 7462 |
| \(P_2\) | 14432 | 14432 |
| \(P_3\) | 1427 | 1427 |

Every entry reduces to Session 10's mod-121 output. Both columns respect \(2H=P_3+P_4+R\). The determinant of the rows \(P_1,H\) is

\equiv 1537\pmod{14641},\qquad 1537\equiv8\pmod{11},
\]
so the local map is again certified surjective.

## 2. Structural improvement: precise location of the local kernel

Working on \(E(K)\otimes\mathbb Z_{11}\), \(2\) is invertible and complex conjugation yields a direct sum of eigenspaces \(V^+\oplus V^-\) of ranks 4 and 1. The rational generators lie in \(V^+\), and \(R\) lies in \(V^-\). The two local logarithm components swap under conjugation, so
\[
\lambda(P_i)=(u_i,u_i),\qquad \lambda(R)=(v,-v).
\]
The exact table shows \(u_1\equiv4\pmod{11}\) and \(v\equiv9\pmod{11}\): both are units. Thus both eigenspace image lines are filled, and
\[
\boxed{\ker(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)\text{ is rank }3\text{ and lies entirely in }V^+.}
\]
This is an **exact** statement about the 11-adic completed Mordell–Weil group, not merely a mod-\(11^4\) rank observation. The whole-kernel claim follows because a nonzero unit on the rank-one anti-invariant summand is injective, and the invariant summand maps surjectively to the diagonal line.

A convenient modular basis is given by \(P_j-c_jP_1\) for \(j=2,3,4\), with \(c_j=u_j/u_1\) in \(\mathbb Z_{11}\). At precision \(11^4\):

| vector | \(c_j\pmod{14641}\) | \(c_j\pmod{11}\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(P_2-c_2P_1\) | 8690 | 0 |
| \(P_3-c_3P_1\) | 14082 | 2 |
| \(P_4-c_4P_1\) | 12835 | 9 |

These represent \(\mathbb Z/11^4\)-linear combinations in the **completed** point group, not integral rational points with literal fractional 11-adic coefficients.

## 3. A falsifiable higher-value conjectural bridge

Let \(a_i=h^{ac}_{11}(P_i,R)\) be the four *genuine global* anticyclotomic 11-adic cross heights, with a specified logarithm branch, idele-class character, splitting, and model differential. These numbers **have not been computed**.

The previous height symmetry proves that, if the vector \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_4)\neq0\), the radical of the first height pairing equals a rank-three hyperplane inside \(V^+\). Today's calculation identifies a **different**, independently defined rank-three hyperplane, the local elliptic logarithm kernel. It would be attractive if the two matched, but we must not assume this.

**Leapfrog conjecture L11 (high risk; unproved):** the two hyperplanes coincide for this curve at 11. The claim is equivalent to \(a\neq0\) and all three projective wedge tests vanishing:
\[
\Delta_{1j}:=u_1 a_j-u_j a_1=0,\qquad j=2,3,4.
\]
A *single* nonzero \(\Delta_{1j}\) **falsifies** L11. These tests are invariant under a common nonzero rescaling of the global height functional and are much better targets than visually comparing matrices. At finite precision and with compatible integral height normalization, alignment would force the ratios
\[
(a_2/a_1,\,a_3/a_1,\,a_4/a_1)\equiv(8690,14082,12835)\pmod{11^4},
\]
assuming \(a_1\) is a unit under the chosen normalization. Without the unit hypothesis, one must work with valuations and the exact wedge tests over \(\mathbb Q_{11}\) instead.

**Important:** There is *no* known theorem established in this notebook forcing L11 to be true. A successful experimental match at finite precision would be evidence only, and a mismatch would redirect the project toward the true global height radical.

Should L11 fail, compute a nonzero \(a_i\), use it to construct the genuine first-height radical, and then compute the alternating second derived height on its three-dimensional space. This is meaningful progress in the *p-adic* BSD framework but is still not a proof of complex analytic rank four.
